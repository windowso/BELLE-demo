Bayesian evidential learning
with language modelling for speech synthesis.

BELLE is a continuous-valued autoregressive TTS model that places a Bayesian prior over the mean and variance of every mel-spectrogram frame and predicts it from the input, so the spread of the speech it generates changes with what is being said.

01 · Abstract

A distribution, not a point.

PRIORNormal-Inverse-Gamma
PARAMSγ, ν, α, β
CORPORAParallelVox · LS+6TTS
REGIMEOne-to-many AR

Text-to-speech (TTS) is a one-to-many problem: one text can be spoken in many valid ways, so its realizations form a distribution whose spread depends on the input. Most TTS models do not model this spread explicitly: they leave it implicit in the sampling process or fix it in advance.

To model this distribution explicitly, we propose BELLE (Bayesian evidential learning with language modelling), which places a Bayesian prior over the mean and variance of each mel-spectrogram frame and predicts this prior from the input, while keeping the architecture and single-pass sampling of common continuous-valued autoregressive TTS models such as MELLE.

Estimating this variance requires several recordings of each text, whereas standard TTS corpora provide one. We therefore construct two parallel-speech corpora: ParallelVox from real human recordings and LS+6TTS, which adds speech from six pre-trained TTS teachers to LibriSpeech (LS).

Trained on these corpora, BELLE outperforms MELLE under the same training conditions, is competitive with larger TTS systems on the objective metrics of LibriSpeech test-clean, and supports streaming generation. Analyses of repeated human readings show that real speech has the properties the Bayesian prior assumes and that the variance predicted by BELLE follows the variation across these readings. Parallel speech improves BELLE, and synthetic parallel speech performs comparably to real parallel speech at the same volume and is far easier to scale.

02 · Method

Model architecture.

Several realizations of each text provide the statistical support for estimating the variance; the Sampling Module predicts the four parameters of a Normal-Inverse-Gamma prior at every frame and samples the frame from it in a single pass.

FIG.01  Overview of BELLE. Left: the mel-based AR TTS architecture. Right: the Sampling Module, which predicts a Bayesian prior over the mean and variance of each frame from the hidden representation et and samples the frame from it in three steps: an Inverse-Gamma draw of the variance, a Gaussian draw of the mean, and a Gaussian draw of the frame.

FIG.02  BELLE-stream interleaves text and mel-spectrogram chunks as the input of the AR model under a causal mask, so speech is generated chunk by chunk while the text is still arriving. It uses the same Sampling Module as BELLE.
